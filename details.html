Sri Krishna Temple

📍 Udupi, Karnataka

🕉️ Main Deity: Lord Krishna

About the Temple

The Sri Krishna Temple in Udupi is one of the most significant Krishna temples in India. Founded by Jagadguru Sri Madhvacharya in the 13th century, it houses a beautiful idol of Lord Krishna that is worshipped through a window with nine holes called 'Navagraha Kitiki'. The temple complex includes several other shrines and the famous Car Street where various religious festivals take place.

Known for its rich historical significance and architectural beauty, the temple attracts thousands of devotees daily. The temple is associated with the Dvaita philosophy propagated by Sri Madhvacharya and is one of the Ashta Mathas (eight monasteries) established by him.

Historical Significance

The temple has a rich history dating back to the 13th century when Sri Madhvacharya, the proponent of Dvaita philosophy, installed the idol of Lord Krishna. According to legend, the idol was originally from Dwaraka and was discovered by Madhvacharya embedded in a lump of gopichandana (sacred clay).

The temple has been an important center for the preservation and propagation of Vedic knowledge and Bhakti tradition. It has been visited by many saints and philosophers over the centuries, contributing to its spiritual significance.

Architecture

The temple complex is built in the traditional Dravidian style of architecture, featuring a main shrine for Lord Krishna, surrounded by smaller shrines dedicated to various deities. The unique feature of this temple is the way Lord Krishna is worshipped through a silver-plated window with nine holes (Navagraha Kitiki).

The temple complex also includes the Kanakana Kindi, a small window through which Saint Kanakadasa is said to have received darshan of Lord Krishna. The temple has intricate stone carvings, wooden pillars, and a magnificent gopuram (tower) that showcase the architectural brilliance of ancient times.

Cultural Impact

The temple is not just a place of worship but also a center for cultural activities. It hosts various religious and cultural events throughout the year, including classical music concerts, dance performances, and religious discourses.

The Paryaya festival, held every two years, is of particular significance. It involves the transfer of the temple management between the eight Mathas established by Madhvacharya. This unique administrative system has been in practice for centuries and is a testament to the temple's cultural heritage.

Festivals & Events

Paryaya Festival (held every two years in January), Krishna Janmashtami, Radhastami, Madhva Navami, and daily rituals such as Darshan, Abhisheka, and Maha Puja are celebrated with great devotion. The temple follows a strict schedule of rituals and ceremonies that have been maintained for centuries.

During Krishna Janmashtami, the temple is elaborately decorated, and special pujas are performed throughout the night. The Car Festival (Rathothsava) is another important event where the idol of Lord Krishna is taken out in a procession around Car Street.

Visiting Guidelines

  • Traditional dress is preferred (dhotis for men, sarees or churidars for women)
  • Photography is restricted inside the main shrine
  • Maintain silence and discipline within the temple premises
  • Mobile phones should be switched to silent mode
  • Follow the queue system for darshan
  • Special archanas and sevas can be booked at the temple office
